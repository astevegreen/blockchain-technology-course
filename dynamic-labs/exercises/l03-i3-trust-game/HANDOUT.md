# The Insider: day 1 lab opener

About 20 minutes, in your browser. Before you read your first block, play a game about agreeing with people
you cannot trust. You install nothing.

## Join

1. Scan the QR code on the screen, or open the address your instructor gives you. It ends in
   `/exercises/insider/`.
2. Type your name and choose **Join open game**. If your instructor gives you a room code, choose
   **Join private game** and enter it.
3. To bring a neighbour into your room, show them the **Invite more players** QR code on your screen.
4. A room holds up to six players and needs at least four, bots included. When your room is ready, anyone can
   choose **Start round**. If your instructor has bots switched on, they fill empty seats up to five.

## Rules

- One player in your room is the Insider, and stays the Insider for the whole game. Your screen tells you
  your role.
- Each round has a new secret word. Everyone sees it except the Insider.
- Give one clue word and choose **Lock in clue**. No two players may give the same clue.
- Vote for the player you think is the Insider. A tied vote removes nobody.
- If the Insider is voted out, they get one final guess at the word.
- The Insider can choose **Guess now** at any time: a right guess wins, a wrong one gives the game to the group.
- **The group wins** if the Insider is voted out and misses the final guess, guesses wrong, drops out or runs
  out of time.
- **The Insider wins** by guessing the word, or by surviving until only three players are left.
- Every step has a timer. Miss it and you are out of the game.
- When a game ends, choose **Return to main menu** to play again.

## What to notice

Note a word or two after each game; the debrief starts from your notes.

1. How much did your clue give away? Proving you are honest can help the Insider.
2. What made you trust or suspect a player: their clue, or how they played?
3. Did your room agree on who to vote out? What changed people's minds?
4. A wrong vote removes an honest player. How did the game change as the room got smaller?
5. What would have made agreeing easier: something you could check, rather than someone you had to believe?
