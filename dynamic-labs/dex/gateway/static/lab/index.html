Day 4 lab: a swap and a bridge

Day 4 has two course chains. The first, L1, holds an exchange built the way Uniswap v2 is: a dollar token (cUSD), wrapped ether (WETH), one pool that holds both, and a router people call to trade [2] [4]. It also holds one half of a bridge. The second chain, L2, holds the other half and the bridged dollar it creates. You read both in Otterscan, the open-source explorer you used on day 1 [1]: this page's address opens L1, and the same address with dex-l2 in place of dex opens L2. Then you make a swap of your own, bridge what you bought, and read both sides.

Write your answers down as short notes. The lab ends in a ten-minute class debrief on them.

If the explorers or the chains are down, use the saved pages. They show the same history, so every question below has the same answer there. For part 2, the saved pages hold a recorded run of the swap page.

Where to start

  • Start with the worked example, after the names on these chains. Your instructor may send you straight to the questions instead; you may always do the worked example first.
  • After the questions, part 2: you swap ETH for cUSD, bridge it to L2, and read both chains.
  • The challenge at the end is optional.

Worked example: a swap in block 9

This walks through one swap, field by field. Questions 1 to 5 use the same pages for block 11.

Find it

  1. Open the L1 explorer.
  2. Type 9 in the search box and press Enter. You are on block 9's page.
  3. Note the block's timestamp: 17 Sep 2026, 14:01:48 UTC.
  4. Open the block's one transaction. Its hash is 0x637c6489f69b169e81feba5895fc9c2f2f0752d2e44d98fe1470ad7838d46fd9.

Read it

From / Nonce0x90F79bf6EB2c4f870365E785982E1f101E93b906 (Account 3), nonce 1
Interacted With (To)CourseRouter: 0xCf7Ed3AccA5a467e9e704C703E8D87F634fB0Fc9
Value0 ETH
Transaction Fee0.00003864323857377 ETH
Input Data0x38ed1739, then the amount in (0x12a05f200, 5,000,000,000), the least it will accept (0), the token path (cUSD then WETH), who receives (Account 3) and a deadline
  • The trader calls the router, not the pool. The router works out the price, moves the trader's cUSD into the pool, and asks the pool to pay out [4]. It holds nothing when the call ends. The pool is the contract that holds both tokens.
  • The pool's two numbers. A pool keeps one balance of each token, its reserves. Which is "0" and which is "1" is fixed when the pool is created: in this pool's code (the Contract tab) the token with the lower address is token 0. Here that is cUSD (0x5FbD… is lower than 0xe7f1…), so reserve 0 is cUSD and reserve 1 is WETH.
  • Four logs. Open the Logs tab. Two Transfer logs: 5,000 cUSD from Account 3 to the pool, written by cUSD; 2.374148687907796351 WETH from the pool to Account 3, written by WETH. Then the pool writes Sync, its reserves after the trade, and Swap: what came in, what went out, and who received [3]. Each amount is scaled by its token's decimals: cUSD has 6, WETH 18 [6].
  • The price is the ratio of the reserves. Before the swap the pool held 100,000 cUSD and 50 WETH: 2,000 cUSD per WETH [2]. Sync says it now holds 105,000 cUSD and 47.625851312092203649 WETH: about 2,204.69 cUSD per WETH. Buying WETH made WETH dearer.
  • The trader's price is worse than either. Account 3 paid 5,000 cUSD for 2.3741 WETH: about 2,106 cUSD per WETH. A pool keeps the product of its reserves from falling, and takes a 0.3% fee on what comes in [2], so a trade that is large next to the pool moves the price against the trader as it fills [5].
  • Who the trader is. The Swap log's sender is the router, not Account 3: the router called the pool. The trader is the address that signed the transaction and received the WETH [3].

A statement from this page: "On 17 Sep 2026 at 14:01:48 UTC, Account 3 sold 5,000 cUSD for 2.3741 WETH through the course exchange's router, moving the pool's price from 2,000 to about 2,205 cUSD per WETH." The amounts and addresses are facts the chain records. That Account 3 traded for itself, rather than for someone else, is a judgment the chain alone cannot settle.

The questions you answer

Some answers are facts the chain records: a hash, an amount, a log. Others are judgments about what the facts mean. Give each judgment a confidence word [11]:

HighTwo or more independent lines of evidence agree, and no known failure of the reasoning applies.
ModerateOne strong line of evidence, or weaker lines that agree, but a known false positive could apply.
LowTiming, amount or behavior alone, or one unverified label such as an explorer tag.

Independent means a different kind of evidence, not a second look at the same transaction.

The swap, on L1:

  1. Block 11 holds a swap. Who sent it, which contract did they call, and which contract holds the pool's money?
  2. How much WETH went in, and how much cUSD came out? Name the logs you read it from.
  3. What did the pool hold just before the swap, and just after? Which of its two numbers is cUSD?
  4. What was the price of one WETH in cUSD before the swap, and after it? What price did the trader get? By how much did the swap move the price?
  5. Write one sentence an agent could read about block 11. Is Account 4 the trader, or could the router or the pool be? Give your confidence and the evidence for it.

The bridge, L1 to L2:

  1. L1 block 14 holds a bridge deposit. Who made it, how much, to which address on L2, and when?
  2. Find the L2 transaction that completed that deposit. Who sent it, who received the money, how much, and how long after the L1 deposit? What links the two transactions? Give your confidence.
  3. Where is Account 5's 3,000 cUSD now? What does Account 8 hold on L2, and which contract created that token?
  4. What kind of bridge is this? Give the evidence from each chain.
  5. Who could stop money crossing this bridge, or freeze it on either side? Give your confidence and say what it rests on.

The problem

Answer the questions above. These hints say where to look.

  1. Who and what. Open L1 block 11's transaction. Compare "Interacted With (To)" with the addresses in the Logs tab: which address sends and receives the tokens? The Trace tab lists the calls the router made inside the transaction; the explorer asks the node for the transaction's call tree [12].
  2. Amounts. Read the two Transfer logs and the Swap log. Scale each amount by its token's decimals, as in the worked example.
  3. Reserves. The pool's reserves before block 11 are the Sync log of the last trade before it: block 9's. After are block 11's own Sync. Reserve 0 is cUSD.
  4. Price. Divide the cUSD reserve by the WETH reserve, before and after. The trader's price is cUSD out divided by WETH in. Give the change as a percentage of the price before [5].
  5. The trader. Count the lines of evidence: who signed, who the WETH came from, who the cUSD went to, and the Swap log's to. Which of them are independent of each other? Remember what the Swap log's sender means.
  6. The deposit. Open L1 block 14 and its second transaction. The bridge wrote a DepositInitiated log: it carries a deposit number, the sender, the address on the other chain and the amount. In a rollup, users deposit into a bridge contract on L1 and the same amount is created for them on L2 [8]. This lab's bridge is simpler, a trusted bridge [7]: one key, the relayer, carries each deposit to L2.
  7. The other side. Open the L2 explorer. The bridge's address is the same on both chains: the address alone does not tell you which chain a page is about, so check the explorer's chain name. Search for the bridge on L2 and look for a finalizeDeposit whose DepositFinalized log has the same deposit number. L2 seals a block every 2 seconds and L1 every 12, so match by time, not by block number.
  8. Holdings. Search for Account 8 on L2: its page lists no transactions. The explorer lists the transactions an address sent or was sent, not the token logs that name it. Read the bcUSD Transfer log in the L2 transaction instead. On the swap page, under "Ask the contracts", ask balanceOf on each chain. Then find the transaction that created bcUSD: open bcUSD's address page on L2 and look at who created it.
  9. The kind of bridge. Bridges lock and mint, burn and mint, or swap with a party on the other side [7]. Ask which one fits what you saw on L1 (where the cUSD went) and on L2 (where the bcUSD came from). Read both bridges' code on their Contract tabs.
  10. Who could stop it. Read on the Contract tabs: setPaused on each bridge, who finalizeDeposit accepts calls from, and cUSD's addBlackList. Then ask who holds each role (the "Ask the contracts" panel reads owner and relayer). Real dollar tokens can be frozen by their issuer [10], and some rollups have councils that can act in an emergency [9].

Part 2: swap, then bridge

You need the live chains and a seat number from your instructor. If the chains are down, answer from the recorded run instead.

  1. Open the swap page and load your seat.
  2. Ask the router for a quote: how much cUSD 0.1 ETH buys now.
  3. Build the swap, read the call the page shows you, then sign and send it. The page waits for the next L1 block and reads your cUSD balance back.
  4. Bridge what you bought: the page approves the bridge, then deposits your cUSD to your own address on L2. Each is a transaction of its own.
  5. Wait for the relayer. The page watches the L2 bridge until your deposit is done, then reads your bcUSD balance there.
  6. Read the panel at the bottom of the page: it lists every question the page asked each node [13].

Answer:

  1. Your swap's hash and block, the cUSD you received, and the pool's price before and after. Did you get the quote? If not, why might the price have moved?
  2. Your deposit's number, its L1 block, the L2 transaction that completed it, and the delay between them. Which account sent the L2 transaction?
  3. The L1 bridge now holds your cUSD, and L2 shows bcUSD at your address. If the relayer had sent a finalizeDeposit with no deposit behind it, what would L2 show, and how would you find out?

Challenge

Optional.

  1. Under "Contract reads" on the saved pages, compare the cUSD the L1 bridge holds with bcUSD's total supply on L2. Should they match? What would it mean if L2's were larger?
  2. Account 2 put the pool's money in (block 7). Who owns the pool's shares now, and what are they worth in cUSD and WETH? Read the pool's totalSupply and balanceOf, and its reserves at block 18 [5].
