Swap, bridge and ask the contracts

This page plays the part of a wallet and an exchange site. It builds each call, signs it with your seat's key and hands it to a node: L1's for the swap and the deposit, L2's to watch the bridged money arrive. The panel at the bottom shows every question the page asks either node, and the answer.

Your seat's key comes from a test phrase printed in public software documentation. It guards nothing and exists only on these training chains.

1. Your account

2. Ask for a quote

The router's getAmountsOut reads the pool's reserves and works out what 0.1 ETH buys now, after the pool's 0.3% fee.

3. Swap 0.1 ETH for cUSD

The page asks for at least 99% of the quote. If the price moves further before your transaction is in a block, the router refuses it.

4. Bridge your cUSD to L2

Two L1 transactions: approve lets the bridge take your cUSD, then depositERC20To locks it there and names your address on L2.

5. Ask the contracts

These are the read-only questions the worksheet asks. The page sends them with eth_call: the node runs the contract's code and answers, and nothing is written to the chain.

What this page asked the nodes

Each line is one JSON-RPC call: /rpc is L1, /rpc-l2 is L2. The method, what was sent and what came back.
