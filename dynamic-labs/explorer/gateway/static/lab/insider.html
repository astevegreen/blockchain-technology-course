Step 1: The Insider

About 15 minutes. A short game that shows, before any technical word is used, what a group has to rely on when nobody can check anybody's word. The rest of today shows how a ledger changes that.

Play

  1. Open The Insider. Your instructor tells you whether to create a room or join an open one.
  2. Everyone but one player is shown the same secret word. One player, the Insider, is not.
  3. Give a one-word clue that proves you know the word without making it easy to guess. If you are the Insider, blend in.
  4. Vote for the player you think is the Insider. If the group is wrong, or the Insider guesses the word, the Insider wins.
  5. Play until the game ends. Some seats may be filled by computer players: you are not told which.

If the game is down, play the card version: your instructor deals one blank card and the rest get the same word, then you give clues aloud and point on the count of three.

The questions you answer

Write short notes. Your instructor checks them with you. Give each judgment a confidence word: High, Moderate or Low, as defined on the main page (the words come from ICD 203 [1]).

1. Who decided who the Insider was, and what was the decision based on?

2. Name the player you voted for. How confident were you, and what was your evidence?

3. Name one way an Insider could have fooled the group even though every player followed the rules.

Where this goes

Today's lessons are about how a network of strangers agrees on a record when no one can be trusted. Keep your notes: after the lessons you will see which of the group's problems a ledger solves and which it does not.
