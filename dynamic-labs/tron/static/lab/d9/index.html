Day 9 lab: a sanctioned deposit address, its sweeps and Tether's freeze

On 30 Sep 2026 the U.S. Treasury sanctioned a financial network of Tren de Aragua, which it says steals cash from U.S. ATMs and "uses cryptocurrency transactions to launder proceeds" [1]. OFAC's list gives seven Tron addresses for the people it named. One of them, TDxZ1XTZCmqkJJW2eJT362z6omRchJyGBX, is listed for Oscar Leonardo Martinez Pirona [2] (both read 1 Nov 2026). This lab reads that address's USDT from 1 Sep to 30 Nov 2023, found through TronGrid's index and read from a java-tron node [3].

You end the day with an exposure summary: what the address is, which stablecoin it held, who issued it, whether it can be frozen and how fast, and which money-laundering typology fits best. Question 15 is the day's graded point (The graded note).

Every query is written for you. On the query page, press "Run" under a query, then read its output. You never edit the SQL. Write your answers down as short notes. Your instructor checks them with you.

The snapshot is frozen: every run gives the same answer. The blacklist and balances in queries 5, 7 and 8 are as of block 87,630,406 (1 Nov 2026, 20:25:03 UTC), when they were read; the chain has moved on since. The balance the address held when the window opens (469 USDT on 1 Sep 2023) is summed from its USDT history since 20 Mar 2022. If the query page will not load, use the saved results: they show every query's output, so every question has the same answer there.

Where to start

  • Most students start with the worked example, then check it with questions 1 to 5. It reviews day 5's deposit and sweep on this address's first two months.
  • If you can already tell a deposit from a sweep, you may go straight to the problem (questions 6 to 10).
  • Everyone then does the freeze and the typology (questions 11 to 14) and writes the graded note (question 15). Questions 6 and 12 each have a self-check box. The challenge at the end is optional.

Reminders from day 5

  • A deposit address is an address an exchange gives one customer account; money sent to it is that customer's deposit. The exchange then moves it, whole, into a wallet that pools many customers' funds: a sweep [4].
  • Report the deposit, not the sweep. The deposit ties the money to one account; the sweep only says which exchange.
  • USDT on Tron is a TRC-20 token at TR7NHqjeKQxGTCi8q8ZY4pL8otSzgjLj6t [5]. A USDT transfer costs Energy, paid by burning TRX when the sender has none [6]. Who sends that TRX is evidence of who controls the address.
  • Times are UTC. Amounts are in USDT or TRX, to six decimals.

How you answer

Some answers are facts the chain records: an address, an amount, a block. A fact takes no confidence word. Others are judgments about what the facts mean. Give each judgment a confidence word [7], never a percentage:

HighTwo or more independent lines of evidence agree, and no known failure of the reasoning applies.
ModerateOne strong line of evidence, or weaker lines that agree, but a known false positive could apply.
LowTiming, amount or behavior alone, or one unverified label such as an explorer tag.

Independent means a different kind of evidence, not a second look at the same transaction. A label is evidence only with its source and the date you read it.

Worked example: deposits and sweeps to Binance (September to October 2023)

This follows the address's first two months, to a wallet whose owner published it. Questions 6 onward use the same queries.

Find it

  1. Open the query page and wait for "Ready". Press "Run" under query 1. Twenty USDT deposits from 11 senders, 92,451 USDT in; 14 outflows to 2 addresses, 92,615 USDT out; first seen 5 Sep 2023, last 27 Nov. The what_a_source_says column gives the OFAC listing, its source and the date it was read: listed 30 Sep 2026, read 1 Nov 2026 [2].
  2. Run query 2. On 5 Sep at 18:29:12, TWnBq4Zeo3U6bwqgdNfxv1gotHJTaAU6Ar deposited 15,000 USDT. The balance after it is 15,469, because the address already held 469 when the window opened. At 18:46:03, 17 minutes later, all 15,469 went out to TV6MuMXfmLbBqPZvBHdwFsDnQeVfnmiuSi and the balance went to 0. Five more times up to 5 Oct, deposits arrive and one outflow empties the address to the same place. An outflow that empties the address to the same place each time is a sweep.

Follow the money

  1. Run query 3. Six sweeps, 58,844 USDT, went to TV6Mu... from 5 Sep to 5 Oct. Binance publishes a list of its own wallets, and it lists TV6Mu... as its USDT wallet on Tron [8] (list updated 9 Oct 2023, read 31 Oct 2026). It is the same wallet as in day 5's lab.
  2. Run query 4. Before each sweep, TYD4pB7wGi1p8zK67rBTV3KdfEb9nvNDXh sent the address 14.5366 TRX, 8 to 10 minutes ahead. The address signed each sweep itself and burned 13.3959 TRX for its Energy. Someone topped it up with just enough TRX to move its USDT out, as day 5's Binance deposit address was.

What you report

  • Up to 5 Oct 2023, TDxZ1X... behaves as a deposit address at Binance, with moderate confidence: its money is swept, whole, to a wallet Binance itself publishes, and a third account pays for each sweep, as a service does for its own addresses. It is not high: both lines come from the same sweep transactions, and no source names TDxZ1X... as Binance's.
  • Report a deposit, not the sweep: for example 15,000 USDT from TWnBq4... on 5 Sep 2023, 18:29:12 UTC, transaction a7db8d43.... The sweep at 18:46:03 is the exchange moving its own customers' money.
  • OFAC's listing is a second, separate fact: the U.S. government ties the address to Martinez Pirona [2]. It does not say which exchange, and the chain does not say who the account holder is. The exchange's records would.

Check yourself

Run each query and check what the worked example says against what you see. Your instructor checks these with you; they are not graded.

  1. Query 1. How many deposits came in, from how many senders, and how many outflows went out, to how many places? What does a published source say about the address, and when was it read?
  2. Query 2, the rows up to 5 Oct 2023. What happens to the balance after deposits arrive? Why is the first outflow (15,469 USDT) larger than the deposit before it (15,000)?
  3. Query 3. Where did those sweeps go? Who published that address, and when?
  4. Query 4. Who paid for each sweep, with how much TRX, and how long before it?
  5. What do you report: which address, which deposit transaction, and which service? Give your confidence and the lines of evidence behind it.

The problem: the sweeps change (22 October to 30 November 2023)

From 22 Oct 2023 the pattern looks the same, but one thing changes. Look at where the sweeps go, at who pays for them, and at what query 3 says about the new address.

  1. Query 2 and query 3, from 22 Oct 2023. Where do the sweeps go now? Does any published source name that address?
  2. Query 4. Who pays for these sweeps? Is it the account that paid for the sweeps to Binance?
  3. Is TDxZ1X... still a deposit address after 22 Oct, and can you say at which service? How far can you carry the Binance label, and with what confidence?
  4. Query 5. Who paid in? Which senders sent the most, and does a published source name any of them?
  5. Query 2, the last two rows. What happened to the 305 USDT that arrived on 27 Nov? What does the chain not show you about who owns this address?

The freeze

Tether, USDT's issuer, can add an address to USDT's blacklist. In USDT's published code, only the contract's owner can blacklist, and a blacklisted address cannot send its USDT [9]. The Tron contract records each addition as an AddedBlackList event, and anyone can ask it whether an address is blacklisted now and what it holds [5]. You read these functions on day 3 and saw a freeze on day 5.

Query 7 is as of block 87,630,406: write "blacklisted as of block 87,630,406, read on 1 Nov 2026", not "blacklisted".

  1. Query 6. What did USDT's contract record on 30 Sep 2026, for which addresses, at what times? Which account signed the transactions?
  2. Query 7 and query 8. As of block 87,630,406, is the case address blacklisted, and how much USDT does it hold? How many days passed between its last sweep in the window and the blacklisting?
  3. Freezable? Which issuer, and how fast? Did the freeze stop any money at this address?

The typology

Question 14 asks you to weigh a typology, not to prove one. The cue in the typology table is a pattern, and a pattern alone is one line of evidence. Day 9's table gives, for example, "OTC and nested services": many small deposits in and a few large transfers out [10], or a service inside an exchange's cluster [11]. "No typology fits: a plain deposit address" is an answer too.

  1. The typology table on day 9's typologies slide gives a cue for each typology. Which typology fits this address best, or does none fit, so that it is a plain deposit address? Name one indicator for your answer and one against it, each from a query or a source.

The graded note

  1. Write the exposure summary of TDxZ1X... for your instructor.

Question 15 is the day's graded point. Your instructor marks it Meets or Not yet, and you get one retry in class time. It meets when it has all four:

  1. The deposit: the address is a deposit address, with one deposit transaction named and told apart from the sweep that followed it, and the service it was swept to, with how far that label goes.
  2. The stablecoin: which stablecoin, which issuer, whether it can be frozen, and how fast the freeze came compared with the money.
  3. The typology: the most likely AML typology, or that none fits and it is a plain deposit address, with one indicator for and one against.
  4. Sources and confidence: each label with its source and the date it was read, and each judgment with a confidence word (never a percentage), including what the chain does not show. A fact read off the chain takes no confidence word.

Challenge (optional)

For early finishers. Questions 16 and 17 each have a flag: an address or an amount you find in the results. Type a flag in its box to check it. The page holds only a fingerprint of each flag, not the flag, and nothing is sent or graded. Your instructor has the flags.

  1. Flag: an address. Query 9. What else arrived at the case address: which tokens, how much, from whom? Did the owner ask for them? Do they tell you anything about who controls the address? The flag is the address that sent the largest number of tokens (the tokens differ, so this is a count, not a value).
  2. Flag: an amount. Query 5 and query 7. One sender, TFSYy3..., is blacklisted today and holds 36,162 USDT, but it is not in query 6's events of 30 Sep 2026. What can you say about it from the snapshot, and what can you not? Another sender, TUpHuD..., holds over 33 million USDT. What kind of address might hold that much, and what would you need before you named it? The flag is the USDT that TFSYy3... sent the case address in this snapshot.
