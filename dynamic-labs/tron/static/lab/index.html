Day 5 lab: a transfer, a sweep, a freeze

About half of all USDT is on Tron: 92 billion of 180 billion, as of 31 Oct 2026 [11]. This lab reads a snapshot of real Tron transactions, found through TronGrid's index and read from a java-tron node [1]: one USDT transfer, two deposit addresses and the sweeps that empty them, and a freeze by Tether, USDT's issuer.

Every query is written for you. On the query page, press "Run" under a query, then read its output. You never edit the SQL. Write your answers down as short notes. Your instructor checks them with you.

The snapshot is frozen: every run gives the same answer. The freeze status and balances in queries 5 and 6 are as of block 87,600,650 (31 Oct 2026, 19:36:21 UTC), when they were read; the chain has moved on since. If the query page will not load, use the saved results: they show every query's output, so every question has the same answer there.

Where to start

  • Most students start with the worked example (questions 1 to 5). It shows you the query page and the pattern you look for.
  • If your pretest showed you can already read a Tron transfer, you may go straight to the problem (questions 6 to 10).
  • Everyone then does the freeze (questions 11 to 14). The challenge at the end is optional.

Reading Tron

  • A Tron address starts with T. It is the Base58Check encoding of a key hash [2].
  • USDT on Tron is a TRC-20 token: a smart contract that keeps every holder's balance, Tron's version of Ethereum's ERC-20 [3]. Its contract is TR7NHqjeKQxGTCi8q8ZY4pL8otSzgjLj6t [5]. A USDT transfer is a call to that contract.
  • Calling a contract costs Energy. An account gets Energy by staking TRX, by having another account lend (delegate) it Energy, or by burning TRX from its own balance when it has no Energy [4]. The query page shows the TRX burned as trx_burned_for_energy.
  • An account with no TRX and no Energy cannot send its USDT. Who pays for a sweep is evidence of who controls the address.
  • Times are UTC. Amounts are in USDT or TRX, to six decimals.

The questions you answer

Some answers are facts the chain records: an address, an amount, a block. Others are judgments about what the facts mean. Give each judgment a confidence word [10]:

HighTwo or more independent lines of evidence agree, and no known failure of the reasoning applies.
ModerateOne strong line of evidence, or weaker lines that agree, but a known false positive could apply.
LowTiming, amount or behavior alone, or one unverified label such as an explorer tag.

Independent means a different kind of evidence, not a second look at the same transaction.

Worked example: a deposit at an exchange (October 2023)

  1. Query 1. Who sent the USDT, who received it, how much, and when? Who signed the transaction, and what did it cost them?
  2. Query 2, address TXLhMxzterktHm9z27XamgXz2EL9EfoBnR. What happens to its balance after deposits arrive? Where does it go?
  3. Query 3. What does a published source say about the address the sweeps went to? Who published it?
  4. Query 4. The address held no TRX of its own. Who paid for each sweep, how, and how long before it?
  5. What do you report: which address, which transaction, and which service? Give your confidence and the lines of evidence behind it.

The problem: a second deposit address (September to October 2026)

  2. Query 4. Who paid for these sweeps, and how is it different from the worked example? How much TRX did each sweep burn?
  3. Query 3. What does a published source say about the address the sweeps went to?
  4. Is TY4zRA... a deposit address at a custodial service? Give your confidence and why.
  5. Which service is it? What would you need before you could name one?

The freeze (23 April 2026)

  1. Query 5. What did USDT's contract record on 23 April 2026, for which addresses, and in which block? Which account signed the transactions?

Worked example: a deposit at an exchange

This follows one USDT transfer into an address, then the address's money out to a wallet that its owner published. Questions 6 onward use the same queries.

Find it

  1. Open the query page and wait for "Ready". Press "Run" under query 1. Open "The query" to see what it asks.
  2. One row. On 16 Oct 2023 at 15:21:15 UTC, in block 55,612,753, TKZsHBGCw8kA3PTkbq4fCt4NNvkrW9uRes sent 400 USDT to TXLhMxzterktHm9z27XamgXz2EL9EfoBnR. The transaction ID is d11ff48c97d846c4a92a94ebdec4ce58bce04028e0c4c6c74d18236337d956bd.
  3. The sender signed it (signed_by), and the type is TriggerSmart: a call to the USDT contract. It used 31,895 Energy. The sender had none, so it burned 13.3959 TRX for it; the whole fee was 13.7409 TRX.

Follow the money

  1. Run query 2 and read the rows for TXLhMx.... Deposits come in from a few senders. Three times the balance goes to 0 in one outflow to the same address, TV6MuMXfmLbBqPZvBHdwFsDnQeVfnmiuSi: 800 USDT on 7 Oct, 655 on 10 Oct and 1,000 on 16 Oct, 25 minutes after our 400. An outflow that empties the address to the same place each time is a sweep.
  2. Run query 3. Binance publishes a list of its own wallets, and it lists TV6Mu... as its USDT wallet on Tron [6] (list updated 9 Oct 2023, read 31 Oct 2026).
  3. Run query 4. Before each sweep, TYD4pB7wGi1p8zK67rBTV3KdfEb9nvNDXh sent the address 14.5366 TRX, 10 to 15 minutes ahead. Each sweep then burned 13.3959 TRX for its Energy. Someone topped up the address with just enough TRX to move its USDT out.

What you report

Report the deposit, not the sweep. A deposit address belongs to one customer account at the exchange [12]; the wallet it is swept into pools many customers' funds [13], so naming that wallet says only which exchange. The sweep is the evidence that ties the deposit address to the exchange. Here:

  • TXLhMx... is a deposit address at Binance, with moderate confidence. Two lines of evidence agree: its money is swept, whole, to a wallet that Binance itself publishes; and a third account pays for each sweep, as a service does for its own addresses. It is not high: both lines come from the same sweep transactions, so they are not independent kinds of evidence, and no source names TXLhMx... itself.
  • The deposit is transaction d11ff48c...: 400 USDT from TKZsHB... on 16 Oct 2023, 15:21:15 UTC.
  • Binance, not the chain, knows who the account holder is.

The problem

Now do questions 6 to 10 for TY4zRAJbq9ZEZvWDHQfJh2AcsiJRPKLeVt. The pattern is the same; two things are not. Look at how the sweeps are paid for, and at what query 3 says.

The freeze

Tether can add an address to USDT's blacklist. In USDT's published code (its Ethereum contract), only the contract's owner can blacklist, and a blacklisted address cannot send its USDT [14]. The Tron contract has the same blacklist functions and events: it records each addition as an AddedBlackList event, and anyone can ask it whether an address is blacklisted now and what it holds [5]. Its owner is a multi-signature wallet, TBPxhVAsuzoFnKyXtc1o2UySEydPHgATto [5] (read 31 Oct 2026).

On 23 April 2026, Tether announced that it had frozen $344 million in USDT across two addresses, with the U.S. Government [7]. Do questions 11 to 14 with queries 5 and 6. Query 6 is as of block 87,600,650: write "blacklisted as of block 87,600,650, read on 31 Oct 2026", not "blacklisted".

Query 5's what_a_source_says column names the sources the course found for each address: a news report naming the two wallets [8], and a list of the addresses on OFAC's sanctions list [9] (read 31 Oct 2026). A blank means the course found no source.
