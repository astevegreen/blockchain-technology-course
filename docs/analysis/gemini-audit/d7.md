# Day 7: Where the trace goes dark

## 1. Verdict
Day 7 provides strong conceptual foundations on obfuscation techniques and realistic limits-of-tracing tradecraft, but the day suffers from structural seams between the morning lectures and the afternoon lab. With targeted fixes to worksheet navigation, rubric consistency, and terminology handoffs, Day 7 will solidly deliver its core analytical learning objectives.

## 4. What holds up
- **Coordinator shutdown and revival timeline**: 06-FO accurately dates the 2024 Samourai and zkSNACKs enforcement actions alongside their 2025–2026 successors (kruw.io, Ashigaru) without overstating law enforcement permanence.
- **PayJoin and dust tradecraft**: 06-FO cleanly articulates why PayJoin breaks co-spend heuristics without visual tells, and why unsolicited dust transactions cannot be imputed to the recipient.
- **Cryptographic trade-offs in 07-FO**: The presentation of Monero ring signatures, stealth addresses, and RingCT accurately separates protocol-enforced privacy from operational leakage at centralized exchange ramps.
- **Cryptojacking characterization in 08-FO**: Differentiating cloud compute victim loss from on-chain attacker revenue, while warning against developer donation wallet false positives, instills sound analytical rigor.
- **Lab environment isolation**: The frozen 40-block chain in `coinjoin-trace` reliably isolates CoinJoins, planted leaks, and custodial mixing without external network dependencies, backed by automated tests against `facts.json`.

## 5. Patterns to check elsewhere
- Lab HTML worksheets placing question prompts before the worked example and detaching hints into separate trailing sections.
- Forensic lessons introducing specialized jargon in labs (e.g., "toxic change") that was never defined on slide decks.
- Lessons claiming to model "the lab product" for topics that the afternoon lab explicitly excludes.
- Graded rubrics stating "Names both..." or restrictive counts that conflict with model answers requiring intermediate steps.
- Limits-of-tracing slide examples conflating multi-asset hopping rules (e.g., citing privacy token delistings for base-layer coin deposits).
