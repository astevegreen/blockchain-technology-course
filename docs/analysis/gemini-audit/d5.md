# Day 5: Exchanges and the rules

## Verdict
Day 5 teaches essential exchange, regulatory, and gatekeeper concepts with strong forensic framing, but suffers from structural scheduling and lab integration defects. The lab entry rule inappropriately routes students past the Tron worked example based on unrelated AML pretest questions, while the afternoon lab underruns its 2 h 35 min slot by more than an hour. Lesson 01-PC attempts to cover both public Tron token mechanics and private enterprise DLT architectures in a single 50-minute lecture, creating severe cognitive overload.

## What holds up
1. The explanation of Tron's resource model accurately shows how an account with zero TRX can transfer USDT via delegated Energy while utilizing free daily bandwidth.
2. Emphasizing the deposit address and transaction over pooled exchange hot/cold wallets provides analysts with legally sound attribution boundaries supported by Coinbase 10-K filings.
3. The 19-of-27 Super Representative threshold for block solidification on Tron correctly captures Byzantine fault-tolerant DPoS finality requirements.
4. Beat 1 of the Hyperledger Fabric demo convincingly proves that chaincode validates cryptographic MSP certificate identity rather than arbitrary payload text.
5. Teaching analysts to differentiate between point-in-time blacklist status (`isBlackListed`) and historical events (`AddedBlackList`) prevents false assumptions about freeze timing.

## Patterns to check elsewhere
1. Lab entry rules tied to conceptual knowledge from unrelated morning lectures rather than the specific procedural skills practiced in the lab.
2. Labs relying solely on pre-written queries that finish in a fraction of their allocated 2 h 35 min blocks without hands-on student investigation.
3. Smart contract owner models in slides omitting the externally owned account (EOA) transaction submitter shown in actual explorer/node query output.
4. Regulatory lectures conflating objective threshold filings (CTRs) with subjective suspicion leads (SARs).
5. Attempting to bridge public permissionless protocols and permissioned enterprise consortiums within a single 50-minute lecture slot.
