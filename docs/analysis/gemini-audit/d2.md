# Day 2: Bitcoin and wallets

### Verdict
Day 2 establishes the core mechanical foundation for transaction analysis and wallet structures, but it suffers from severe cognitive overloading in lesson 02-BF and flawed deduction prompts in the multisig lab. While lessons 05-BF and 06-BF maintain strong analyst discipline, the lab worksheet actively misleads students regarding multisig signature ordering and lacks a worked example for its complex second half. Removing obsolete legacy files and fixing worksheet prompts will stabilize the day for a cohort of newcomers.

### What holds up
1. Single-sig address derivation and explorer verification in Part 1 perfectly mirror BIP 84 test vectors and prove that on-chain addresses do not reveal common ownership.
2. The distinction between on-chain proof (keys signed) and off-chain inference (people holding keys) is strictly maintained across 06-BF and lab Q11.
3. The offline mempool explorer interface cleanly handles local-time conversion, unrounded virtual sizes, and hidden witness metadata via the Details toggle.
4. Challenge question (a) faithfully implements the BIP 44 gap limit (20 unused addresses, indices 3–22), requiring a concrete analyst remediation.
5. Dana's accountant xpub question accurately tests hardened derivation boundaries, proving that account xpubs cannot reach independent hardened branches like the treasury.

### Patterns to check elsewhere
1. Overstuffed morning lecture slots packing multiple disparate mechanisms to compensate for retired introductory modules.
2. Lab worked examples demonstrating only initial basic mechanics while leaving the complex multi-step core problem unexemplified.
3. Student worksheet hints prompting students toward non-existent deductions or phantom clues.
4. Silent discrepancies between custom local course testnets and standard mainnet consensus rules.
5. Obsolete legacy drafts remaining in lesson directories while referenced ambiguously in run sheets.
