# Day 10: Post-test, then a final trace

## 1. Verdict
Day 10 delivers a technically impressive, realistic dual-chain capstone lab supported by robust offline fallback pages and faithful adherence to ICD 203 confidence standards. However, the day suffers from severe operational bottlenecks: the afternoon schedule cannot accommodate oral assessments for 20 to 30 students, and the lunch window is physically too short to mark 240+ written post-test responses. Furthermore, the answer key contains a mathematical contradiction on its cross-chain decoy, and the case file omits the OTC desk fee required to calculate the expected payout.

## 4. What holds up
- The dual-chain architecture seamlessly coordinates Bitcoin bitcoind blocks and Tron DuckDB snapshots to build an authentic cross-chain tracking narrative.
- Saved fallback pages for both chains accurately duplicate live explorer and database outputs, ensuring zero classroom downtime if Docker services fail.
- The confidence rubrics strictly enforce ICD 203 terminology (High, Moderate, Low) while penalizing probabilistic percentages and unwarranted certainty.
- The Bitcoin clustering problem realistically models multi-input co-spending and round-amount change detection without artificial simplifications.
- Distinguishing unfreezable Bitcoin change from freezable Tron USDT and pooled exchange deposits reinforces actionable asset-recovery analysis.

## 5. Patterns to check elsewhere
- Lab schedules across all days that allocate flat assessment times without scaling for classes of 20 to 30 students.
- Case files that omit operational real-world parameters (exchange withdrawal fees, OTC spreads, slippage) needed to identify downstream hops.
- Cross-day container dependencies that assume continuous background Docker execution across overnight classroom pauses.
- Instructor-only challenge banks that lag curriculum additions and omit late-course concepts.
