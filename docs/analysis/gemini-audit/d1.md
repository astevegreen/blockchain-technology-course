# Day 1: Reading the ledger

## Verdict
Day 1 establishes foundational cryptographic and ledger principles, but suffers from a severe pedagogical disconnect between its morning lectures and afternoon lab. While the morning lessons teach Bitcoin transactions and the UTXO model, the afternoon lab drops newcomers into an EVM explorer and demands analysis of smart contracts, ERC-20 token logs, and execution reverts two days before they are taught. Compounding this, the lab is overloaded with extraneous tooling (a Linux terminal multiplexer and an AI party game), and lecture notes are riddled with stale cross-references to obsolete day schedules.

## What holds up
1. The mathematical walkthrough of Alice's Bitcoin coffee transaction in 02-IN perfectly reconciles inputs, outputs, fee, and miner coinbase rewards.
2. The cryptographic treatment of secp256k1 key generation, discrete log resistance, and address derivation across Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Tron in 04-CP is rigorous and accurate.
3. The explanation of hash function properties (pre-image resistance, second pre-image, avalanche effect, Keccak-256 vs NIST SHA3-256) is clear and directly relevant to data integrity.
4. The consensus comparison matrix in 01-CM accurately contrasts finality mechanisms across PoW, PoS checkpoints, Tron Super Representatives, and Arbitrum sequencer feeds.
5. The standalone containerized architecture of the course chain with frozen fallback pages guarantees lab determinism even if network services fail.

## Patterns to check elsewhere
- Stale forward references in lecture notes left over from prior syllabus reorganizations (e.g., old day numbers for mixers, bridges, and ransomware).
- Lab exercises that introduce and test technical concepts ahead of their scheduled morning lecture slots.
- Divergence between master schedule documents (RUN_SHEET.md) and individual lesson slide tables regarding session start times and durations.
- Substantive lessons that omit interactive check-for-understanding questions in speaker notes.
- Low-level developer artifacts (RPC methods, code compilation details) presented without filtering for the analyst mission.
