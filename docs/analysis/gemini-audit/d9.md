## What holds up
- The firm separation between observable on-chain characterization and official government attribution prevents analysts from overstepping into speculative claims.
- The anti-echo rule in Lesson 14-FO effectively stops analysts from treating multiple vendor tags originating from the same OFAC notice as independent lines of evidence.
- The frozen DuckDB-WASM snapshot design provides a reliable, self-contained environment that shields locked-down student laptops from network RPC failures.
- The worked example in the Tron lab clearly distinguishes initial customer deposits from subsequent automated exchange sweeps.
- Evaluating Tether blacklists against historical transaction timing trains analysts to distinguish between actual asset freezes and performative, delayed designations.

## Patterns to check elsewhere
- Temporal ordering conflicts where reshuffled lessons retain speaker notes referencing adjacent slots out of sequence.
- Discrepancies between theoretical confidence criteria taught in morning lectures and the marking rubrics applied in afternoon labs.
- Synthetic database views dropping metadata columns (such as dates, block heights, or transaction hashes) that the worksheet prompts explicitly ask students to report.
- Labs forcing real-world transaction patterns into narrow academic typology taxonomies.

## Could not check
- Live classroom delivery pacing of the student exercises in 13-FO, 12-FO, and 14-FO within their 50-minute slots without empirical pilot timing data.
- Browser-specific rendering and DuckDB-WASM query execution performance across restricted government agency host configurations without live machine testing.
