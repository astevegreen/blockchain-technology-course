# Day 4: DEXs, Layer 2s and bridges

## Verdict
Day 4 provides strong quantitative foundations in constant-product automated market maker pricing and an elegant dual-chain bridge lab. However, it suffers from contradictory bridge taxonomies across lectures and exercises, misleading lab hints that set students up to fail confidence scoring, and an unannounced pool contention bottleneck where simultaneous student swaps revert. Conceptual coherence is further compromised by sandwiching Solana and account abstraction internals into already overloaded 50-minute lessons.

## What holds up
- Constant-product formulas and numerical price impact calculations in `07-EF` exactly match Uniswap v2 invariants and fee deductions.
- Anvil test seat key derivation in `swap.js` cleanly isolates 40 classroom seats without hardcoding private keys or risking cross-seat nonce collisions.
- The Python bridge relayer daemon idempotently tracks processed deposit IDs on L2, ensuring resilience against container restarts and reorgs.
- Look-alike guidance in `03-EF` clearly differentiates routers, aggregators, and MEV sandwich bots from actual investigation targets.
- The lab worksheet turns Otterscan's lack of ERC-20 transfer indexing into an effective teaching exercise on reading raw contract event logs.

## Patterns to check elsewhere
- Taxonomy drift: establishing a strict categorization on an introductory slide, then introducing ad-hoc types in case studies and lab worksheets.
- Hidden lab grading traps: instructor keys docking confidence marks for missing multi-transaction context that worksheet hints never told students to seek.
- Unannounced environment contention: shared-singleton lab services where simultaneous class actions cause routine reverts without student-facing explanations.
- Illogical syllabus placement: wedging standalone recognition topics into the middle of complex architectural lessons rather than grouping them at natural breaks.
- Inexact exit vocabulary: conflating on-chain asset swaps with actual ledger exits or fiat off-ramps in tracing methodology.
