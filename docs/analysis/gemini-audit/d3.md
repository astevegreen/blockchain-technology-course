# Day 3: Ethereum and tokens

## Verdict
Day 3 delivers exceptionally strong conceptual modeling for stablecoins and logs, particularly the Tether blacklist and the failure modes of log-only balance indexers. However, the day is severely compromised by Lesson ② attempting to teach a full semester of compiler and language theory in 50 minutes, and the token lab depending on a fragile singleton container that cannot survive an overnight restart without losing all student credentials and badges. Pruning developer-centric syntax from Lesson ② and persisting container state are necessary before this day can succeed in front of novice analysts.

## What holds up
1. The forensic contrast between `transfer` and `transferFrom` in 05-EF accurately explains how drainers exploit allowances without breaking protocol rules.
2. The Tether `destroyBlackFunds` demonstration in the lab exposes the exact blind spot of commercial indexers that sum `Transfer` logs alone.
3. The block 3 worked example walks students through 32-byte padded calldata, decimals, and logs without forcing them to write code.
4. Distinguishing EOA signatures from contract message calls establishes the right mental model for tracing funds through routers and bridges.
5. The freeze evaluation rule ("The analyst flags; the agent acts") cleanly enforces analyst role boundaries without legal process drift.

## Patterns to check elsewhere
1. Developer-oriented lessons overshooting analyst requirements by teaching compiler internals, opcodes, and multiple foreign languages in 50 minutes.
2. Fragile multi-day lab container state where nightly host reboots wipe cumulative student progress prior to Day 10.
3. Disconnects between required grading rubric evidence (e.g. storage slot reads) and the capabilities of the bundled open-source explorer.
4. Speaker notes containing `Walkthrough: None` and lacking interactive "Ask:" check questions across Days 1 to 4.
5. Multiple nested Quarto div fences (`:::: {.content-hidden}`) creating parser ambiguities during site generation.
